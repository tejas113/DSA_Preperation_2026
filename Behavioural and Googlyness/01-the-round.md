# The Behavioral Round – SWE L3

Loop: **R1 Behavioral** → R2 Technical → R3 Technical. There is **one** behavioral round.

At Google, the SWE behavioral round is officially called **"Googleyness & Leadership" (G&L)**.
So this single round is where Googleyness gets scored. It covers everything:

| What happens | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Intro | "Tell me about yourself", "Why Google?" |
| Past-experience questions | "Tell me about a time…", resume / project deep-dive |
| Hypotheticals | "Imagine that…", "What would you do if…" |
| Your questions | Last ~5 minutes |

**Typical shape:** ~45 minutes, about **4–6 main questions**, each followed by several follow-ups
("What did *you* do?", "Why?", "What would you change?", "What if they pushed back?").

**Scored on:** Googleyness (ambiguity, bias to action, team-first, aiming high, intellectual humility,
choosing the harder path) + emergent leadership, with some RRK/GCA from how you explain your work.

## What L3 means for expectations
- Nobody expects you to have led big teams. "Leadership" at L3 means **initiative**: unblocking someone,
  proposing a better approach, owning a bug end-to-end, helping a new teammate onboard.
- One main project is fine. **A story is a moment, not a project**, and one project holds many moments.
- Stories from your other project, college, hackathons, or life outside work are valid too (aim for ~7 of 10 from work).

## Delivery rules
- 2–3 minutes per story. Situation in **under 30 seconds**.
- "I" for your actions, "we" for the team's. Be precise about the boundary.
- Real numbers if you have them. "Roughly", "around" is honest and fine. Never invent.
- End with what you learned or what you do differently now.
- Hypotheticals: understand and find the root cause → options with pros and cons → take a stand → success metric → stay open.
- Don't reuse the same *moment* for two questions in the round.
