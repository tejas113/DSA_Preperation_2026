## STAR
**S – Situation**
- About [one month] before our v1 release, the team changed a lot within a few weeks: architects rolled off, interns finished, and my intern moved to another company. **We went from 7 MLEs to 3.**
- We had my parser and a fairly brute-force prototype for rule application, and a lot still unproven.

**T – Task**
- Deliver v1 with three people. I owned **manuscript parsing, front-matter and body-matter rule application**. My senior handled back matter, reference validation and deployment.

**A – Action**
- A new architect joined and brought real structure: she pulled in **testers to check manuscripts rigorously**, which surfaced many more patterns. I fed those edge cases straight into **hardening the parser**.
- As a team, we **rewrote the style-guide rules into a form an LLM can follow reliably**. [Explain one sentence: e.g. "breaking vague rules into explicit conditions and examples".]
- I [kept my areas moving / prioritized what v1 truly needed / documented. Add what *you* did to keep things on track].
- I watched how the architect handled it: **calm, never angry, aligning everyone even when things weren't going well**. I deliberately learned from that, and even asked her how she balanced it all.

**R – Result**
- In about [one month] we built the tool end to end: **parse manuscript → apply rules → JSON output → UI renders the edits**, and **delivered v1**.
- **Lesson:** in a crisis, structure beats heroics: rigorous testing, clear ownership, calm communication. I also learned sustainable work habits from the architect instead of relying on late nights.
