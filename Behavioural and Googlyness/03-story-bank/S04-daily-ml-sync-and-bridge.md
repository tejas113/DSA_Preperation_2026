# S04 – Started a daily ML sync and became the bridge between onshore and offshore
**Source:** current project · **Answers:** C1 ⭐, C7, E8, B7, C4 · real example for F24 (time zones)
**Googleyness shown:** emergent leadership, team-first, bias to action

## STAR
**S – Situation**
- Our ML team was split between the US and India. There was no ML-specific sync, only a general evening huddle, and people logged off right after.
- Findings and approaches stayed in silos. I was mostly talking to the onshore architect, and the offshore side often didn't know what had been tried.

**T – Task**
- Setting up team process wasn't my job; I was the junior. But I could see we were losing time and repeating work, during a discovery phase that was the best time to explore.

**A – Action**
- I proposed to our offshore ML lead that we hold a **short daily ML sync** to share findings, approaches and blockers. My argument was that discovery is exactly when we should be learning fastest, not waiting on the client.
- I took on being the **bridge between onshore and offshore**: overlapping hours, carrying context across, and **writing my observations into shared docs** so nothing depended on one conversation.
- Before v1, when the team still wasn't aligned on the approach, I **didn't escalate it as a complaint**. I went to the senior teammates one by one, walked them through how I saw the approach, and **asked them to talk directly** with the architects so they were aligned without me in the middle.

**R – Result**
- The team agreed and the daily sync started. [Is it still running? Any concrete effect, e.g. "decisions that used to take days happened in the sync"?]
- Alignment improved, and it's when the offshore team started noticing my work.
- **Cost and lesson:** being the bridge meant some late nights. I learned that **I shouldn't be the single point of communication**. Shared docs and getting people to talk *directly* scale; one person relaying doesn't.

## Anchors
