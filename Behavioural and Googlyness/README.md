# Google Behavioral (Googleyness & Leadership) Prep

Interview loop: **R1 Behavioral** → R2 Technical → R3 Technical.
The behavioral round *is* Google's "Googleyness & Leadership" round, so everything below happens in that one round (updated 2026-10-13).

Goal: walk in with a bank of *real* stories I know deeply, not a script. I should be able to tell
any story in 2–3 minutes and survive 5 levels of "why?" follow-ups.

---

## What they're actually scoring

Google's 4 hiring attributes (every round contributes to the packet the hiring committee reads):

| Attribute | What it means | Where it shows up for me |
|---|---|---|
| RRK – Role-related knowledge | Right skills/experience for the role | Project deep-dives |
| GCA – General cognitive ability | How I think, learn, break down hard problems | How I reason in hypotheticals |
| Leadership (emergent) | Stepping up *without the title* when my skills are needed, then stepping back | Leadership stories |
| **Googleyness** | Culture *add*, not just culture fit | The whole behavioral round |

**Googleyness dimensions** (ex-Google hiring manager's breakdown):
1. **Thriving in ambiguity**: acting decisively when things are unclear
2. **Bias to action**: deciding with incomplete info; a doer, not only a thinker
3. **Collaborative, team-first**: making the team smarter matters more than being the smartest person in the room
4. **Moonshot / 10x thinking**: aiming beyond "realistic", real excellence
5. **Intellectual humility**: admitting I was wrong and learning from it
6. *Hidden thread:* **choosing the harder or courageous path**: calculated risks, betting on myself, being uncomfortable in order to grow

## The 4 mistakes that sink people
1. **Too polished**: sounding rehearsed makes them doubt the story is real
2. **Overclaiming**: "I did it all" gets probed hard. Say clearly what was *me* and what was *the team*
3. **No real failure**: a success dressed up as a failure, or a weakness spun into a strength
4. **Playing it safe on hypotheticals**: take a stand, give reasoning, stay open to pushback

## Answer shape: SPSIL (a skeleton, not a script)
- **S**ituation: under 30 seconds, only the context the story needs
- **P**roblem: what was at stake
- **S**olution: what *I* did and *why*, including the options I rejected
- **I**mpact: numbers where honest, qualitative where not
- **L**esson: what I do differently now (required for failure stories)

---

## How we avoid scripting
- Stories are stored as **anchors only**: 5–8 bullet keywords plus hard facts (dates, numbers, names of tools, what the other person said). **No full sentences or paragraphs.**
- Every story gets a **depth layer**: likely follow-ups and my honest answers ("What would you do differently?", "What did your teammate think?", "Why not X?").
- Mocks use **random question order** and **re-angled questions**, so I get used to adapting one story to different questions instead of reciting it.
- If a story starts to sound memorized, re-tell it from a different starting point (start from the lesson, or from the other person's point of view).

## The Plan

### Phase 1 – Mine raw experiences → `02-raw-experiences.md`
Claude interviews me about everything (internships, jobs, college projects, hackathons, clubs, team
fights, deadlines blown, things I'm proud of, things I regret). Messy brain-dump, no polishing.
Target: **15–20 raw moments.**

### Phase 2 – Map to signals → `04-coverage-matrix.md`
Grid: stories × (Googleyness dimensions + question categories: conflict, failure, leadership-without-title,
ambiguity, difficult person, late project, data-driven decision, creative solution, change, end-to-end ownership).
Find gaps and dig for more stories to fill them.
Pick **8–10 core stories**, each able to cover 3+ questions.
Never reuse the same *moment* for two questions in the round.

### Phase 3 – Shape stories → `03-story-bank/`
One file per story: anchors, my exact contribution vs team's, numbers, the depth layer, and which
questions it answers. Honesty check on every claim.

### Phase 4 – Non-story questions
- `05-intro-and-why-google.md`: "Tell me about yourself" (60–90s arc) and "Why Google?" (specific and personal, not "scale and impact")
- `06-hypotheticals.md`: "What would you do if…" (teammate isolating themselves, disagreeing with manager, competing visions, convincing a team to adopt new tech, unethical shortcut, deadline vs quality). Practice *reasoning out loud*, not memorizing answers.
- Strengths/weaknesses, why leaving, career direction, favorite Google product

### Phase 5 – Mock interviews → `07-mock-logs/`
Claude plays the interviewer: 45 min, 3–5 questions, relentless follow-ups, then feedback on:
- I vs we balance · length (target 2–3 min) · overclaiming · sounding scripted · whether the lesson is real · which Googleyness signals came through
I **speak answers out loud first** (phone recorder), then type or transcribe for review.
Each mock mixes intro, past-experience questions, and hypotheticals, like the real round.

### Phase 6 – Final days
Light review of anchors only. Reread `08-red-flags-checklist.md`. Prepare 2–3 genuine questions to ask the interviewer. Sleep.
